## How priority will be decided

Use four pieces of evidence together:

1. **Historical importance:** your tier is the starting prior.
2. **Personal gap:** repeated misses, unfinished coverage, slow methods or untested competence.
3. **Transfer value:** repairs that help several question families outrank isolated tricks.
4. **Repair cost and likely payoff:** a short, broadly useful repair may earn a place even in a lower tier; an obscure subtopic does not get unlimited time because one mock exposed it.

These are judgment factors, not a calibrated numerical formula. Three old mocks are a small sample. A topic's absence from them means it is untested, not unimportant.

## Starting QA preparation allocation

The following percentages are **our provisional practice/repair budget**, not asserted exam shares:

| Area | Starting share of QA preparation | Application to Srishti's evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arithmetic | 45% | Interest and mixtures diagnostic repairs; ratios/percentages as prerequisites; speed-distance/time-work; fix long calculation paths |
| Algebra | 30% | Starts immediately alongside arithmetic: linear/quadratic equations, inequalities and polynomials; existing linear-equation and quadratic losses justify early work |
| Geometry | 12% | Maintain coverage and compress methods on correct-but-slow triangle/mensuration questions |
| Number Systems | 8% | Target accessible factors, remainders, divisibility and related gaps; cap the isolated surd repair |
| Modern Math | 5% | Maintain accessible skills; use small diagnostic samples to find quick gains; postpone expensive obscure variants |

About three quarters of QA preparation therefore goes to Arithmetic and Algebra together. Algebra does **not** wait until every arithmetic weakness is repaired. Raise a topic's allocation if fresh evidence shows easy gains or a prerequisite bottleneck, and reduce it once the repair transfers reliably.

The existing split of roughly two thirds targeted repair and one third discovery is a second dimension: apply it within this weighted selection. Do not put the entire discovery stream into whichever high-difficulty questions happen to be available. Both streams must retain meaningful Algebra exposure.

For a 20-question QA day, an approximate mix is nine Arithmetic, six Algebra, two or three Geometry, one or two Numbers and one Modern Math question, alternating the rounded counts across days. Repair/transfer questions count within the total. A difficult foundational repair can legitimately reduce the day's question count.

### First diagnostic batch, revised

Use **eight** unseen questions: two interest, two mixtures, two ratio/percentage questions and two linear/quadratic-equation questions. Repair what the attempts expose, including prerequisites, then solve fresh checks. This adds Algebra immediately to the earlier six-question Arithmetic-only starter.

## Starting DILR preparation allocation

DILR labels overlap heavily. Use these mutually assigned practice buckets to prevent double counting; give each practice set one primary bucket according to the reasoning it actually needs.

| Practice bucket | Starting share of DILR preparation | What to include |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grouping/conditionality and related arrangements | 30% | Prioritize conditional grouping; include seating/ordering as the Tier 2 component; explicitly verify constraints |
| Hybrids, caselets with conditions and reasoning-based DI | 30% | Translate constraints into tables/equations; practise representation and deductions, not only arithmetic |
| Games & Tournaments | 20% | Start diagnostic sets immediately; these mocks do not establish competence or weakness here |
| Routes, networks and scheduling | 10% | Regular discovery and targeted repairs |
| Pure charts, observation, Venn/radar and other puzzles | 10% | Rotation for basic competence, efficient calculation and occasional quick gains |

These percentages are practice-budget choices. They are not new claims about CAT frequencies. If a hybrid set also involves scheduling, assign one primary bucket for counting and retain both skill tags for diagnosis.

**Classify by reasoning, not appearance.** A table or bar graph can contain a high-priority conditional hybrid. IMS's broad 'Groupings & Arrangements' tag also does not distinguish your Tier 1 grouping from Tier 2 seating. The full setup must be read before assigning a precise priority label. The current mock records retain their original platform tags; no unsupported historical recategorization has been made.

Over the next ten DILR practice sets, aim for roughly three grouping/arrangement sets, three hybrid/reasoning-DI sets, two games/tournament sets, one routes/scheduling set and one rotating lower-tier set. This is a rotating quota, not ten sets to complete in a single day. Reattempts count toward the matching bucket.

## What changes in the current repair queue

- **Keep interest and mixtures early**, because repeated personal non-conversion and Tier 1 Arithmetic agree. Ratios and percentages move earlier whenever they are prerequisites.
- **Bring Algebra into the first session.** The old mocks cannot justify leaving a critical 20-30% area until every arithmetic topic is done.
- **Keep the overlooked arrangement sets as short diagnostic checks**, because their cohort accessibility and near-zero inspection make them informative. Then broaden to conditional grouping and hybrids rather than drilling only the exact old set format.
- **Add Games & Tournaments discovery now.** Absence from the three mocks is a coverage gap in the evidence, not a reason to omit a Tier 1 family.
- **Cap the surd repair.** Establish the relevant identity/sign reasoning, solve fresh checks, and return to higher-priority material. The 11:35 failure demands a persistence-rule fix; it does not make surds the centre of preparation.
- **Review the slow table/bar-graph sets once for representation and decision errors.** Repeated practice depends on the actual reasoning family: a conditional hybrid can justify substantial follow-up; a pure calculation set belongs in the smaller maintenance allocation.
- **Preserve the VARC diagnosis.** No new VARC frequency reference was supplied. RC maintenance, summary repair and protected coverage remain evidence-based priorities; the QA/DILR percentages are not applied to VARC.

## Preparation priorities versus exam selection

Historical frequency guides what to prepare. During a mock, choose by **your solvability, likely marks and time cost in that actual paper**. A short familiar lower-tier question can be the right first-pass choice. A Tier 1 label cannot justify getting trapped in a set or a long algebra problem. No topic family guarantees its presence or difficulty in CAT 2026.

After two fresh mocks and the first repair cycle, reweight using observed transfer, time cost and actual coverage. Preserve your historical reference as the prior; update the personal-gap evidence as it changes.
