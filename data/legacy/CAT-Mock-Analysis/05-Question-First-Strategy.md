# Question-first CAT strategy

For Srishti. Updated 16 October 2026 after confirming the preferred learning method: solve questions first, then repair what they reveal. No prerequisite full-syllabus course or self-maintained error log.

## The governing rule

**Attempt -> diagnose -> repair the smallest missing piece -> solve a fresh variation -> check again later.**

Your historical topic-frequency tiers and the mock evidence jointly determine what to practise. See [06-Weighted-Priority-Framework.md](06-Weighted-Priority-Framework.md) for the full reference and current allocations. The mocks do not establish that every skipped topic is weak, nor cover the entire syllabus. Use a smaller, frequency-weighted discovery stream to find untested gaps.

During learning, a question is a diagnostic tool. During a mock, it is an investment competing with every other question for limited time. A productive learning struggle can be a disastrous exam decision. The 11:35 surd attempt and the 26:59 DILR set are examples of why these modes need different rules.

## 1. QA: two streams, both starting with questions

Initially give QA about half of focused preparation time, DILR about a third, and VARC the remainder. These are starting allocations, not a fixed timetable. Revise them after a fresh baseline and the first repair results.

Within QA, start with **45% Arithmetic, 30% Algebra, 12% Geometry, 8% Numbers and 5% Modern Math** of preparation effort. These are chosen study allocations, not exam-weightage claims. Arithmetic and Algebra run in parallel from the first session. Both targeted repairs and discovery questions follow this weighted mix over multiple sessions.

If ratio or percentage knowledge blocks mixtures, repair that prerequisite immediately rather than forcing the nominal order. Geometry and surd mistakes receive bounded repairs; they do not displace the two critical areas merely because their old timings were dramatic.

### Stream B: discovery, about one third of QA work

Use mixed unseen questions across arithmetic, algebra, numbers, geometry and modern mathematics, with about three quarters of QA preparation allocated to Arithmetic and Algebra together. Start at accessible-to-medium level. Their purpose is to expose blind spots absent from these three mocks, not to maximize a drill score by repeating familiar chapters.

For an unfamiliar family, try a representative question before opening its lesson. If you cannot form a method, that question has done its job: use a short explanation or worked example, then return to solving. Do not wait for repeated mock failures before learning an obviously missing prerequisite.

### The repair loop for each diagnostic question

1. **Try independently.** For a single QA learning question, give a first attempt roughly three minutes. If no usable representation or first equation emerges, stop the blind struggle and ask for a minimal hint. If the method is sound and you are progressing, finish it; learning time is not the exam cap.
2. **Classify only what the evidence supports.** Is the obstacle an unknown concept, a missing pattern, an inefficient method, an algebra/calculation mistake, or the decision to persist? I will maintain that classification. An incorrect answer alone does not distinguish them.
3. **Repair narrowly.** Learn the exact relation, representation or technique that was missing. A five-to-fifteen-minute concept detour is appropriate when needed; watching an entire chapter by default is not.
4. **Close the explanation and solve again.** Merely understanding a displayed solution does not count as being able to produce it.
5. **Solve two unseen checks:** one nearby transfer question and one with a changed condition. Correctness and a coherent method matter before speed. If these fail, revisit the repair rather than adding more random volume.
6. **Revisit after roughly 48 hours**, then in a mixed batch several days later. A memorized original answer is not the test; an unseen or meaningfully changed problem is.

The transfer questions count toward the day's total. Do not interpret this as 25 diagnostic questions plus another 50 compulsory questions.

### The next concrete QA session

Start with eight diagnostic questions: two interest, two mixtures, two ratios/percentages, and two linear/quadratic-equation questions, all unseen if possible. Try them before explanations. Use the resulting repairs and fresh checks to fill the session. End with a short weighted discovery batch. The total volume depends on how many real gaps appear; completing reliable transfer matters more than reading a large number of solutions.

## 2. DILR: solve first, but measure different skills separately

Across practice sessions, start with **30% grouping/conditionality and related arrangements; 30% hybrid/caselet/reasoning DI; 20% games/tournaments; 10% routes/networks/scheduling; 10% rotating lower-tier families**. These are our preparation shares. Give each set one primary bucket to avoid double counting overlapping labels. Include games/tournaments immediately: their absence from the three mocks leaves your competence untested, not established.

A table or chart can contain high-priority conditional reasoning. Read the setup before calling it pure calculation-based DI. Likewise, IMS's combined arrangement/grouping label does not map cleanly onto the supplied tiers. The old slow table is a bounded diagnostic repair, not automatically a reason to spend most future practice on charts.

The three most useful checks are already identified by the mocks:

| Check | Material | What to learn from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selection | SimCAT 2 Q19-22, then Pre1 Q11-14 | Could an overlooked set actually have been solved by you? |
| Correct setup | SimCAT 2 Q1-5 | Is there one mistaken constraint/case behind several wrong responses? |
| Efficient representation | SimCAT 2 Q6-9 table | Can a clearer table or equation structure avoid 27 minutes of work? |

For a learning set, attempt before looking at the solution. At about four minutes, name the deductions or structure you have obtained. If there is no usable progress after a substantial independent attempt, ask for the smallest next-step hint. Learning the missing representation is the repair. After a solution has been seen, test the method on another set of the same underlying structure, not just the remembered original.

Add a **selection-only exercise**: inspect all five sets in an untouched paper, rank them mentally and choose the first two without solving the full section. Later compare that choice with solvability and cohort evidence. This directly trains the skill that the mocks show is being missed. Do not open an intended fresh full mock merely to do this; use a separate unused sectional or take-home paper.

For arrangements, check every original condition against the completed setup before answering the dependent questions. For DI, test the interpretation with one simple consistency check before doing the remaining calculations. These checks target the specific pattern of clustered errors seen here.

## 3. VARC: repair a narrow weakness while maintaining the strong section

Use the four previously wrong summary questions as diagnostic material, provided you have not already memorized their explanations. First select an answer and state the central claim in one sentence. Then explain exactly why each close alternative changes the scope, emphasis, certainty or causal relationship. I will identify the repeated failure mode from that reasoning.

Follow the repair with fresh summary questions. Start for accuracy; add timing once the option reasoning is reliable. In the next full mock, summaries can remain a provisional later-priority subtype while this repair is incomplete. That is not a permanent rule to skip them.

Maintain RC with passages and questions, not a wholesale replacement of your reading method. Reattempt the skipped Pre2 Q5-8 block and the late Sim2 Q19-21 questions to test whether the old skips were personally difficult or consequences of time allocation. Remember that rereading a known passage makes the reattempt a learning exercise, not a fresh timed benchmark.

## 4. Exam execution: test these rules in the next full attempt

**QA:** inspect all 22 questions in roughly three minutes. On the first solving pass, leave when no usable method emerges after about a minute. Reassess at two minutes of solving; about 2:30 is the initial exit guardrail unless only concrete final steps remain. Finish a first pass by about minute 25, then return to the best parked questions. These guardrails are to prevent six-to-twelve-minute commitments, not to make you abandon a valid solution mechanically.

**DILR:** inspect all five sets, including the last one, before committing. Check for actual deductions around four minutes into a set. Reassess opportunity cost around 10-12 minutes and choose consciously whether the remaining payoff justifies continuing. Verify shared setups before answering a cluster. The rule is not 'drop every set at eight minutes'.

**VARC:** keep the successful core approach, but check remaining coverage around minute 28. Avoid a five-minute stalemate on an individual VA item. Do not impose the same short cap on an RC passage-opening question that includes reading.

## 5. Starting volume and mock cadence

A reasonable initial full-study-day target is **20-25 QA questions including repair/transfer questions; two DILR sets plus one short set-selection exercise; and two RC passages plus four VA questions, including two summaries**. This is a workload to calibrate, not a quota that outranks learning. If a day exposes several foundational gaps, do fewer originals and complete the repairs. If material is fluent, increase mixed timed work.

Start with **two full mocks per week**, each followed by this assistant-led analysis and targeted repairs. Take a fresh baseline soon, after one or two initial repair sessions, because the old attempts are spread over months. Increase full-mock frequency only when you are completing the repairs and can see that selection changes are transferring. Repeatedly taking papers while leaving the same concepts and decisions untouched would reproduce the current losses.

No detailed hourly schedule has been imposed. The point is a repeatable question-and-repair cycle, not filling every available hour.

## How we will decide the strategy is working

Look for more concepts transferred to unseen questions, fewer long stalled commitments, full inspection of QA positions and DILR sets, and accurate chosen-set setups. Judge scores relative to each paper's cohort as well as raw marks. Revisit the time allocation after two fresh mocks and the first repair cycle. Your old scores do not supply a current forecast or guarantee of 99+.
