## The next-mock experiment

This is a test protocol to evaluate, not a proven permanent strategy or a full daily timetable.

### QA

1. Reserve the first roughly three minutes to inspect all 22 questions. Use the on-screen review flag for promising questions; no handwritten tracking sheet is required.
2. First pass: solve questions with a method you can state. If after 45-60 seconds you still cannot form a usable approach, move on.
3. Reassess at two minutes of active solving. Continue a little longer only when the remaining steps are concrete and short; otherwise park it. On the first pass, use about 2:30 as an exit guardrail. The purpose is to eliminate 6-12-minute commitments, not rush every valid calculation.
4. Complete the first pass by about minute 25, then choose from the remaining promising questions. No question in the final third should remain unseen merely because of its position.
5. If no method is available on many questions, the remedy is syllabus work. A timer cannot create missing knowledge.

### DILR

1. Inspect all five sets in roughly three minutes, including their questions. Rank them mentally by a concrete starting representation and manageable constraints, not familiarity of topic name alone.
2. Pick the clearest route. At four minutes, check whether your setup is producing deductions. If it is still speculation without a usable structure, switch.
3. At around 10-12 minutes, explicitly compare the remaining payoff with another inspected set. This is a decision checkpoint, not an automatic abandonment of a nearly finished set.
4. After building an arrangement, verify every original condition before propagating answers across its questions.
5. At minute 20, reassess your second-set route. A set reaching 20+ minutes requires an explicit reason; momentum alone is not one.

### VARC

Keep your successful overall approach. Put a checkpoint around minute 28 to protect a meaningful look at remaining passages/VA. Treat summaries as a provisional last-priority subtype until the four prior errors are repaired. On an individual VA item, reassess after about two minutes; avoid another five-minute stalemate. Do not apply a two-minute cap to a passage-opening RC question that includes the reading itself.
