## Diagnosis

This attempt's main issue was leaving attainable material unseen while paying for low-return work. QA accuracy on attempted Type A questions was excellent, but that accuracy applied to only nine of fourteen available Type A questions.

The raw QA score rose from 27 in PreSimCAT 1 to 30 here, while its mock percentile was lower, 73.39 versus 78.43. Papers and cohorts differ; more marks did not mean stronger relative performance. These attempts were also spread over months, so this comparison is not evidence of a recent trend.

### VARC: RC coverage was sacrificed; summaries failed again

RC returned **25 marks in 25:23**. VA returned **16 in 14:20**. Six non-summary VA questions were all correct, producing 18 marks in 8:53. The summaries Q23-24 then took **5:27 for -2**.

The Q5-8 passage block received only **1:33** and all four questions were skipped. Two were Type A, Q6 and Q7, with zero recorded time. This is a passage-coverage decision, not evidence that application or detail questions are inherently weak topics for you.

The other passage blocks returned 5 marks in 7:07 (Q1-4), 12 in 7:34 (Q9-12) and 8 in 9:09 (Q13-16). These unequal returns matter more than advising you to increase all RC attempts indiscriminately. Preserve the accurate VA work, protect enough time to inspect all passage blocks, and fix summary option evaluation.

### DILR: three blocks entered, but returns were badly uneven

- Q1-5 caselet: **15 marks in 10:35**, all correct.
- Q6-9 bar graph: **4 marks in 14:33**, two wrong and two correct.
- Q10-14 arrangement: **8 marks in 9:59**, with three correct, one wrong and one skipped.
- Q15-18 arrangement: **0:14**, no attempts.
- Q19-22 puzzle: **5:01**, no attempts or marks.

The bar-graph block's first two responses cost 10:58 and -2 marks. Across the section, Type B questions consumed **14:08 for a net zero**. This is not fixed by merely choosing more questions. It calls for checking the interpretation, equations or calculations before continuing with dependent answers.

The last puzzle did not take longer than its benchmark total of 5:52. Calling that abandonment intrinsically bad would be unjustified. The larger failure was that the section had already lost so much time and accuracy in the bar graph. Q10 also deserves review: it had 7:19 attributed to it but was left unanswered even though three neighbouring questions in its block were correct. Some of that time may be shared setup, so it must be reviewed at set level.

### QA: knowledge on chosen easy questions was adequate; access to the rest was poor

Nine Type A attempts were all correct, returning 27 marks. Five Type A questions were skipped: **Q14 interest, Q15 ratios, Q17 mixtures, Q19 polynomials, Q22 remainders**. Four of these had zero recorded time; Q14 had 2:10.

Q15-22 all show zero time. You spent 6:05 solving Q1 linear equations and 4:57 solving Q9 speed-distance. That is **11:02 for 6 marks**. The two TITA errors, Q7 and Q8, took 6:10 and had no penalty, but their time still mattered. Q12 linear equations took another 3:08 and was skipped.

This is why improving only your attempt accuracy would miss the point. The chosen Type A questions were already 100% accurate. You needed faster methods where available, earlier exits where they were not, and an inspection of the back of the paper. Your low syllabus coverage means some unseen questions may still need concept learning before they become selectable.

## What to test next

1. Rebuild the bar-graph set's setup and trace Q6 and Q7 against the data. Determine whether the two errors share one assumption or are independent calculations.
2. Reattempt QA Q1 and Q9 with a clean method, not a remembered answer. Check whether a shorter representation exists.
3. Use QA Q14, Q15, Q17, Q19 and Q22 to identify unfinished concepts. Do not infer ignorance from zero time alone.
4. Retry the skipped RC block before solution exposure. That tests whether the original skip reflected passage difficulty for you or time lost elsewhere.

Reaching 99 on this paper required a broader improvement, including DILR and/or VARC. 'Just do the easy questions' is therefore incomplete advice.
