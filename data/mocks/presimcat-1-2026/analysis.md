## Diagnosis

This was the strongest of the three recorded attempts, but it concealed losses in both DILR selection and QA execution. VARC supplied 57 of 119 marks. DILR and QA together supplied 62. That balance is much healthier than SimCAT 2, yet it still left a 17-mark gap to this mock's displayed 99th-percentile threshold.

### VARC: strong comprehension, a specific summary failure

RC produced **44 marks in 22:12**. All 13 Type A RC questions were correct. VA produced **13 marks in 17:38**. The two summaries, Q23 and Q24, consumed **7:53 for -2 marks**; Q24 alone took 5:36. This is a much narrower problem than weak verbal ability.

Q17, a parajumble, was wrong after 2:34 but carried no penalty. Q10, a detail question, was wrong after 0:37. A single fast detail error cannot establish a general reading weakness. The summary pair deserves the first verbal repair effort because it cost both marks and substantial time.

Do not impose a single short timer on every RC question: the four passage-opening questions include reading time. At the passage-block level, the recorded times were Q1-4 4:18, Q5-8 5:19, Q9-12 5:19 and Q13-16 7:16. All four blocks produced positive returns. The first three returned 12, 12 and 8 marks; the last returned 12.

### DILR: the score is respectable; the selection is inefficient

The scatter-plot block Q6-10 returned **15 marks in 11:46**. The caselet Q1-5 returned **11 in 10:23**, despite an incorrect first response. The second arrangement block Q15-18 returned **9 in 7:46**.

The important comparison is between two other blocks: Q11-14 received **0:15** and no attempts; Q19-22 absorbed **9:57** and also yielded no attempts or marks. All four Q11-14 questions were Type A. In the question-wise analytics, benchmark attempts were 96.0-98.2% and benchmark accuracy 94.30-95.50%. This was a high-priority missed opportunity by cohort evidence, not merely an easy label assigned by me.

The actionable error is spending almost ten minutes on an unproductive set while giving another set almost no inspection. It does not prove that you personally could have solved the skipped set. A blind reattempt is required for that conclusion.

### QA: both accuracy and coverage failed

Four of seven attempted Type A questions were correct. Three Type A questions were skipped. Meanwhile, three Type C questions were attempted, two correctly. This shows that harder-looking material is not universally beyond you, but it does not justify giving it priority over the whole paper.

- Q3, Progressions: **5:49, wrong, -1**.
- Q9, Percentages: **7:12, correct, +3**. A correct answer can still be an expensive exam decision.
- Those two questions together used **13:01 for 2 marks**, roughly one third of the section.
- Q15, Triangles: **3:49, correct** versus a displayed benchmark of 1:41: a possible method-efficiency issue, not proof of a concept gap.
- Q12, Mixtures: 0:11, skipped; Q17, interest: 0:02, skipped; Q20, Factorials: 0:00, skipped. All were Type A.
- Q18-22 each show 0:00. That is evidence of missing recorded coverage, not five independently diagnosed concept failures.

Given your later clarification that very little QA is covered, the repairs must include learning the unfinished concepts. Selection alone will not produce the missing marks.
